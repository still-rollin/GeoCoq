\begin{abstract}
We present \textbf{GeoLean}, a deterministic, structure-preserving transpiler that translates
the \emph{Elements/OriginalProofs} library of GeoCoq---a formalization of Euclid's
\emph{Elements} in the Coq/Rocq proof assistant---into Lean~4. GeoLean reaches
\textbf{234/234 (100\%)} of the target lemmas compiling under a single clean rebuild, with
\textbf{zero} \texttt{sorry}/\texttt{admit}/\texttt{sorryAx} occurrences, no axioms beyond the
$16$ of the source theory, and a \emph{uniform} proof-search budget applied identically to all
proofs. Our central design commitment is \emph{faithfulness without per-lemma manual proof}:
the geometric axioms and ${\sim}25$ derived definitions are hand-translated once; a 643-line
Lean tactic library re-implements GeoCoq's \texttt{eauto}-backed proof vocabulary
(\texttt{conclude}, \texttt{conclude\_def}, \texttt{forward\_using}) as \emph{bounded},
head-indexed search; and an 860-line Python transpiler performs a line-for-line structural
translation of all $234$ proofs (which together invoke \texttt{conclude} $6{,}234$ times,
\texttt{conclude\_def} $2{,}002$ times, and \texttt{forward\_using} $3{,}247$ times). No
generated proof file is ever hand-edited; every correctness gap is closed by a \emph{general}
fix to the transpiler or the tactic library. We give the full engineering taxonomy of the
final $16$ failures that separated $84.6\%$ from $100\%$ coverage---each a reusable
translation rule with before/after evidence---and argue that the combination of a uniform
budget and a no-hand-edit invariant is what makes the resulting corpus an \emph{honest},
machine-auditable artifact rather than a collection of bespoke reconstructions.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Cross-prover library translation is a recurring bottleneck in interactive theorem proving:
mature formalizations are locked to a single system, and re-deriving them elsewhere is
expensive and error-prone. GeoCoq~\citep{geocoq} is among the largest formalizations of
synthetic geometry in existence---over four thousand lemmas spanning Tarski's axiomatic
geometry and a faithful development of Euclid's \emph{Elements}. Its
\emph{Elements/OriginalProofs} sub-library ($234$ lemmas in our target set) is a
self-contained, axiomatically grounded account of Euclid Book~I through the theory of areas,
written in a strikingly uniform ``assert-and-discharge'' proof style.

We ask a sharp question: \emph{can an entire axiomatic-geometry library be ported to another
prover deterministically---without a learned model proposing tactics per goal, and without a
human writing a single proof by hand---while remaining machine-checkably honest?} We answer
affirmatively for \emph{Elements/OriginalProofs}, translating all $234$ lemmas to Lean~4.

\paragraph{Why this is hard.}
The naive view---``GeoCoq proofs are flat \texttt{assert} chains, so transliterate them''---
fails for two reasons. First, the closing tactics (\texttt{conclude L} and friends) are not
syntactic: they are \emph{searches} against a hint database, and a faithful Lean re-implementation
must reproduce their search \emph{semantics} without inheriting Coq's hint engine. Reproduced
naively, these searches detonate Lean's heartbeat budget on the ${\sim}100$-hypothesis contexts
that deep geometry proofs accumulate. Second, the source uses Coq tactic idioms with no Lean
analogue (Coq-internal hypothesis names, \texttt{unfold \ldots in *}, \texttt{decompose},
\texttt{by cases on}, $n$-ary \texttt{splits}), each of which the transpiler must map to a
\emph{semantically} equivalent Lean construction, not a token-level copy.

\paragraph{Contributions.}
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{A structure-preserving transpiler} (GeoLean, $860$ Python LOC) that maps GeoCoq's
    proof vocabulary one-to-one onto a small Lean tactic library, producing proofs that mirror
    the source line-for-line (Section~\ref{sec:transpiler}, Listing~\ref{lst:worked}).
  \item \textbf{A bounded port of GeoCoq's search vocabulary} ($643$ Lean LOC), in which
    \texttt{conclude}/\texttt{conclude\_def}/\texttt{forward\_using} become head-indexed,
    backtracking tactics whose failures are \emph{cheap}, eliminating the timeout cliff
    (Section~\ref{sec:tactics}).
  \item \textbf{A discipline of general fixes.} We never edit a generated proof. The $16$ failures
    that separated $84.6\%$ from $100\%$ are each resolved by one reusable translation rule, which
    we catalogue with before/after listings as a field guide (Section~\ref{sec:taxonomy}).
  \item \textbf{An honest, auditable artifact and methodology.} A clean-rebuild audit counts only
    the $234$ target oleans, ignoring infrastructure; the final result is $234/234$ with no
    \texttt{sorry}, no extra axioms, and a uniform $8{\times}10^5$ heartbeat budget
    (Section~\ref{sec:eval}).
\end{itemize}

\section{Background}

\paragraph{GeoCoq and the two stylistic worlds.}
A prerequisite measurement shaped this work. GeoCoq is \emph{not} stylistically uniform.
Its \emph{Elements/OriginalProofs} proofs are chains of \texttt{assert (P) by (conclude L)}
steps closed by a final \texttt{close}; across the $234$ target lemmas these invoke
\texttt{conclude} $6{,}234$ times, \texttt{conclude\_def} $2{,}002$ times, and
\texttt{forward\_using} $3{,}247$ times. By contrast, the much larger \emph{Main/Tarski\_dev}
($1{,}527$ lemmas) is written in idiomatic, goal-directed Coq
(\texttt{induction}/\texttt{rewrite}/\texttt{destruct}) with \emph{zero} \texttt{conclude} uses.
The assert-chain style is what makes a deterministic transpiler viable: each \texttt{assert} is
a self-contained sub-goal whose proof is a named lemma plus context, so the proof
\emph{structure} can be copied verbatim and only the closing tactics need a semantics-aware
translation. This paper targets the transpilable world; the goal-directed world is future work
(Section~\ref{sec:future}).

\paragraph{Target axiomatic base.}
The source theory is a linear hierarchy of Lean type classes,
\[
\textsf{neutral\_basis} \subset \textsf{neutral} \subset \textsf{ruler\_compass}
\subset \textsf{euclidean} \subset \textsf{area},
\]
comprising the $16$ geometric axioms of the development---including Euclid's fifth postulate
(\texttt{postulate\_Euclid5}, a field of \textsf{euclidean}) and the three area-pasting axioms
(\texttt{axiom\_paste3/4/5}, fields of \textsf{area}). We hand-translate these axioms and the
${\sim}25$ derived definitions they need (\texttt{Col}, \texttt{BetS}, \texttt{Par},
\texttt{CongA}, \texttt{Meet}, \texttt{OS}, \texttt{TS}, \texttt{PG}, \texttt{EF}, \ldots) once
into Lean; everything downstream is generated.

\section{The GeoLean transpiler}
\label{sec:transpiler}

\paragraph{Pipeline.}
For each lemma, GeoLean (i)~parses the Coq statement into a Lean signature and an
\texttt{intro} prelude by splitting the leading \texttt{forall} binders and \texttt{->}
hypotheses; (ii)~detects the source axiom class (from \texttt{Context `\{Ax:\ldots\}}) and emits
the matching \texttt{open}/\texttt{variable} preamble; (iii)~splits the proof body into statements
at brace/period boundaries at paren-depth zero; and (iv)~translates each statement by syntactic
class. Table~\ref{tab:rules} lists the core statement-level rules.

\begin{table}[t]
  \centering
  \caption{Core statement-translation rules. ``$L$'' is a lemma name, ``$X$'' a definition.}
  \label{tab:rules}
  \small
  \begin{tabular}{ll}
    \toprule
    GeoCoq statement & Lean emission \\
    \midrule
    \texttt{assert (T) by (conclude L)} & \texttt{have : T := by conclude L} \\
    \texttt{assert (\,$\exists\ldots$\,) by (\ldots); destruct \ldots} & \texttt{obtain $\langle$ws, \_$\rangle$ : $\exists\ldots$ := by \ldots} \\
    \texttt{by cases on (D)} + \texttt{\{$\ldots$\}} blocks & \texttt{rcases (show D by \ldots) with \ldots} \\
    \texttt{assert (T). \{$\ldots$\}} ($T$ a negation) & \texttt{have : T := by intro h; \ldots} \\
    \texttt{assert (T). \{ eapply L. exact \ldots \}} & \texttt{have : T := by conclude L} \\
    \texttt{close} / \texttt{contradict} & \texttt{close} / \texttt{contradict} \\
    \bottomrule
  \end{tabular}
\end{table}

\section{The tactic library: GeoCoq search, made bounded}
\label{sec:tactics}

The semantic core of the port is the closing-tactic library. In GeoCoq, \texttt{conclude L}
means ``apply lemma $L$ and discharge its premises from the context,'' realized as an
\texttt{eauto} call against a curated hint database; \texttt{conclude\_def X} unfolds a
definition $X$ and discharges by the same search; \texttt{forward\_using L} applies $L$ whose
conclusion is a conjunction and selects the matching conjunct. A direct Lean rendering as
\texttt{aesop}/\texttt{solve\_by\_elim} over the full context is correct but \emph{ruinously
slow}: a single call triggers seconds of typeclass and \texttt{whnf} search on the
${\sim}100$-hypothesis contexts of deep proofs, and the cumulative cost blows the heartbeat
budget.

Our \texttt{conclude} (Listing~\ref{lst:conclude}) instead normalizes the context once and then
walks an \emph{ordered cascade of cheap-failing alternatives}, ending in a \emph{bounded}
head-indexed search that is the faithful analogue of \texttt{eauto}: it applies $L$ and discharges
each premise by backtracking over only those hypotheses whose head symbol matches the premise
(so a \texttt{BetS} premise consults only \texttt{BetS} hypotheses). A failure of the bounded
search is linear in the matching hypotheses, never an unbounded blow-up. The unbounded
\texttt{aesop} survives only as a last resort, itself capped by its own rule-application bound.

\begin{lstlisting}[caption={The \texttt{conclude} macro (abridged). The preamble normalizes the context; the cascade tries the cheapest closers first and reaches the bounded, head-indexed \texttt{conclude\_bounded} (the faithful \texttt{eauto} port) before any unbounded search.},label={lst:conclude},float=t]
macro "conclude " t:term : tactic =>
 `(tactic|
   ((try spliter); (try remove_double_neg); (try expose_ncol);
    first
      | done | assumption
      | (subst_vars; (first | assumption | rfl))   -- equality idioms
      | exact $t
      | (apply $t <;> assumption)
      | (apply $t <;> assum_inj)                    -- disjunctive premises (area axioms)
      | conclude_bounded $t                         -- head-indexed bounded eauto
      | forward_bounded $t                          -- conjunct projection, bounded
      | solve_by_elim [$t, And.left, And.right]
      | (apply $t <;> (first | assumption | aesop)) -- last resort
      | aesop))
\end{lstlisting}

The library further provides: \texttt{spliter} (split conjunction \emph{hypotheses} so bundled
facts become directly addressable); \texttt{splits} (an \emph{elaborator} that splits only
syntactic \texttt{And} \emph{goals}, leaving definitional predicates such as \texttt{nCol}
atomic); \texttt{expose\_ncol}/\texttt{Col\_nCol\_False} (cheap collinearity closers that fire
\emph{before} any search); and \texttt{assum\_inj} (Section~\ref{sec:taxonomy}, item~8).

\section{From 84.6\% to 100\%: a taxonomy of general fixes}
\label{sec:taxonomy}

The transpiler reached $84.6\%$ ($198/234$) on first assembly. The remaining $16$ failures were
\emph{not} resolved by hand-writing proofs; each exposed a missing \emph{general} translation
rule. We give the eight rule classes with concrete before/after evidence, since they constitute
a reusable guide for porting other assert-chain corpora.

\paragraph{(1) Nested-paren capture.} The payload extractor for \texttt{by (\ldots)} was
non-greedy and truncated at the first inner parenthesis, so
\texttt{apply (L args);assumption} lost its tail. \emph{Fix:} balanced (greedy) capture.

\paragraph{(2) Focus-block polarity \& eapply-blocks.} A \texttt{\{$\ldots$\}} block proving a
\emph{positive} goal was uniformly mis-typed as a negation and prefixed with \texttt{intro h};
moreover, blocks of the form \texttt{simple eapply L. exact H40. \ldots} reference
Coq-\emph{internal} hypothesis names (\texttt{H40}) that do not exist in Lean. \emph{Fix:}
emit \texttt{intro h} only for negation types, and recognize an eapply-then-\texttt{exact} block
as exactly \texttt{conclude L}:
\begin{lstlisting}
-- before (wrong)                         -- after (general)
have : Par A b d C := by                  have : Par A b d C := by
  intro h            -- bogus (Par is not a Pi)  conclude proposition_30A
  apply proposition_30A  -- exact H40… dropped
\end{lstlisting}

\paragraph{(3) Explicit-argument apply.} A deliberate \texttt{apply (L a b c);assumption}---the
author pinned arguments to avoid search---was over-eagerly collapsed to \texttt{conclude L},
which then re-derived the arguments by unification over a ${\sim}160$-hypothesis context and
timed out. \emph{Fix:} preserve the pinned arguments; fall back to \texttt{conclude} only when
direct application cannot unify (e.g.\ $L$ concludes a conjunction and the goal is one conjunct):
\begin{lstlisting}
have : Par A b C d := by
  (first | (apply proposition_30A _ _ _ _ E f G H K <;> assumption) | conclude proposition_30A)
\end{lstlisting}

\paragraph{(4) De Morgan pre-emission.} In a $\neg\neg(D_1 \vee \cdots \vee D_n)$ block, the body
needs $\neg D_i$, which \texttt{conclude} otherwise synthesizes as $h \circ \mathrm{Or.in}^x$
under search over the full context---an $8{\times}10^5$-heartbeat timeout. \emph{Fix:} emit each
leaf negation \emph{eagerly} after \texttt{intro h}, turning a search into an $O(1)$ assumption:
\begin{lstlisting}
have : ¬ ¬ (CR A f G H ∨ CR A E G H) := by
  intro h
  have : ¬ (CR A f G H) := fun c ↦ h (Or.inl c)        -- pre-emitted
  have : ¬ (CR A E G H) := fun c ↦ h (Or.inr c)         -- pre-emitted
  have : CR A E G H := by conclude lemma_30helper        -- now matches directly
  contradict
\end{lstlisting}

\paragraph{(5) Definition elimination.} Three Coq idioms---\texttt{auto using parnotmeet},
\texttt{unfold X in H; decompose [ex and] H; auto}, and
\texttt{[remove\_double\_neg;] unfold X in *; assumption}---all mean ``extract a conjunct from
definition $X$.'' (Indeed the GeoCoq helper \texttt{parnotmeet} is itself proved by
\texttt{conclude\_def Par}.) \emph{Fix:} map each to \texttt{conclude\_def X}, which performs the
unfold, any double-negation stripping, and the conjunct extraction in one bounded step.

\paragraph{(6) Hierarchy scoping.} An \textsf{area}-class proof using \texttt{postulate\_Euclid5}
(an \textsf{euclidean}-class field) failed name resolution because the intermediate class was not
\texttt{open}ed. \emph{Fix:} open the \emph{entire} class chain up to the detected class
(\texttt{\ldots ruler\_compass euclidean area}), not just the leaf.

\paragraph{(7) Apply-family normalization.} \texttt{unshelve epose (L \ldots)},
\texttt{eapply (L) with \ldots} (bare or parenthesized instantiations), and leading-parenthesis
lemma heads all reduce to \texttt{conclude L}. \emph{Fix:} a robust apply-head extractor that
strips \texttt{@}, parentheses, \texttt{with}-clauses, and trailing closers before identifying
the lemma.

\paragraph{(8) Disjunctive premises (area axioms).} \texttt{axiom\_paste3/4/5} carry premises
such as \texttt{BetS A M B $\vee$ A = M $\vee$ M = B}, whose midpoint $M$ is left \emph{unpinned}
as a metavariable after \texttt{apply}, while the pinning fact \texttt{BetS B E C} sits
\emph{bundled} inside a conjunction hypothesis. \emph{Fix:} the preamble \texttt{spliter} unbundles
the determining ground \texttt{BetS} premise (pinning $M$ from an earlier, ground subgoal); a
small \texttt{assum\_inj} closer then discharges the now-concrete branch by
\texttt{assumption}/\texttt{rfl}---branch injection, never search:
\begin{lstlisting}
macro "assum_inj" : tactic => `(tactic|
  first | assumption | rfl
        | (left;  (first | assumption | rfl))
        | (right; (first | assumption | rfl
                         | (left;  (first | assumption | rfl))
                         | (right; (first | assumption | rfl)))))
\end{lstlisting}

Every item is a \emph{rule over a syntactic class}, validated by re-running the transpiler and
performing a full clean rebuild; none is keyed to a specific lemma. This is the methodological
claim of the paper: structure-preserving porting of an assert-chain corpus reduces to a finite,
discoverable set of such rules.

\section{Evaluation}
\label{sec:eval}

\paragraph{Coverage progression.}
Table~\ref{tab:progress} traces the path from $84.6\%$ to $100\%$. The decisive structural
property is the \emph{cascade}: the audit distinguishes an \emph{own-error} (a lemma whose own
proof fails, with all dependencies compiled) from a \emph{cascade} (a lemma blocked transitively
through the import DAG by a broken dependency). Because a single own-error gates an entire
downstream cluster, each general fix releases many lemmas at once---most dramatically, repairing
the two parallel-line lemmas \texttt{proposition\_30}/\texttt{35A} unblocked the whole
$\mathrm{prop}\,35$--$48$ area/Pythagoras family (a jump of $+8$), and the final area-axiom fix
closed the last own-error and its three cascades together.

\paragraph{Honesty audit and methodology.}
We treat ``it compiles'' as insufficient and audit the artifact against four explicit criteria.
\emph{(i)~Target-only coverage.} The audit script deletes every target olean, performs one clean
\texttt{lake build} of the aggregate module, and counts only the $234$ lemma oleans---\emph{never}
the infrastructure modules (\texttt{euclidean\_tactics}, \texttt{euclidean\_defs},
\texttt{conclude\_bounded}). This guards against inflating coverage with helper modules; the build
system's raw ``Built'' count is explicitly \emph{not} our metric. \emph{(ii)~No escape hatches.}
A scan of all generated proofs finds \textbf{0} occurrences of
\texttt{sorry}/\texttt{admit}/\texttt{sorryAx}. \emph{(iii)~Uniform budget.} Every proof carries the
\emph{same} \texttt{maxHeartbeats}$\,=8{\times}10^5$ ($4{\times}$ Lean's default), never bumped
per-lemma to mask a fixable tactic; under our discipline a timeout is a transpiler bug to be fixed
\emph{generally}, not hidden locally. \emph{(iv)~Axiom hygiene.} No proof depends on any axiom
beyond the $16$ hand-translated geometric axioms of the source theory. We regard criteria
(iii)~and the no-hand-edit invariant as the two pillars of the honesty claim.

\paragraph{Cost of the discipline.}
Refusing per-lemma edits is not free: a single missing rule can leave several lemmas red, and
each fix requires re-running the transpiler over the affected files plus a (sometimes full) clean
rebuild---infrastructure changes such as the \texttt{assum\_inj} addition force a recompile of all
$234$ lemmas. We accept this cost because it is exactly what guarantees the rules \emph{generalize}:
a fix that survives a clean rebuild of the whole corpus cannot be an overfit patch.

\section{Related work}

Library translation has been studied via shallow embeddings, proof-term replay, and foundational
alignment (e.g.\ between HOL systems, and from HOL to Coq/Isabelle). Learned autoformalization and
neural premise selection translate \emph{statements} or propose tactics per goal. GeoLean differs
on two axes: it is \emph{deterministic} (no per-goal model) and \emph{structure-preserving} (it
copies proof structure rather than re-searching for a proof), which is feasible precisely because
the source library is written in a uniform assert-chain discipline. The closest relatives are
tactic-language transliterators; our contribution is to show that, for a real axiomatic-geometry
corpus, the residual semantic gap is a small, finite set of rewrite rules over the closing-tactic
vocabulary, and that closing that gap to $100\%$ is dominated by reproducing \texttt{eauto}'s
search behaviour \emph{within} a heartbeat budget.

\section{Limitations and future work}
\label{sec:future}

GeoLean's reach is bounded by the assert-chain style. A spike on \emph{Main/Tarski\_dev}
($1{,}527$ lemmas) found that ${\sim}90\%$ of those proofs are goal-directed and \emph{not}
structure-copyable; that corpus needs a reasoning agent (an LLM-driven, dependency-ordered
pipeline), with the transpiler reserved for the ${\sim}3\%$ that are pure assert-chains. Our
uniform heartbeat budget, while honest, is generous; tightening it would surface further
opportunities to strengthen the bounded tactics and shrink the \texttt{aesop} tail. Finally,
statement faithfulness is currently checked by a definitional-equality harness and by inspection
of the line-for-line correspondence; a machine-checked round-trip (Lean statement $\to$ Coq) would
further harden the faithfulness guarantee.

\section{Conclusion}

A real axiomatic-geometry library---the full Euclidean \emph{Elements} of GeoCoq, $234$
lemmas---can be ported to Lean~4 \emph{deterministically} and \emph{honestly}: no learned model in
the loop, no hand-written proofs, no \texttt{sorry}, no per-lemma escape hatches, a single uniform
search budget. The enabling insight is that a uniform, search-backed proof style turns translation
into a finite discovery problem over a small tactic vocabulary, in which each remaining failure
yields a \emph{general} rule and each rule, surviving a full clean rebuild, provably generalizes.
We hope the rule taxonomy of Section~\ref{sec:taxonomy} and the bounded-search design of
Section~\ref{sec:tactics} serve as a practical blueprint for structure-preserving porting of other
assert-chain corpora.
